LORDSFAVOUR PLAYER OF THE YEAR — 100-POINT EVALUATION FRAMEWORK
1. Purpose
This framework evaluates the best football player of a given year using a standardized 100-point system.
The objective is not simply to identify the player with the most goals or the player whose team won the most trophies. The framework measures five dimensions:
Team success
Personal statistical performance
Match-winning impact
Match-winning efficiency
Discipline
The same framework should be applied every year. Only the underlying season data changes.

3. CATEGORY I — TEAM SUCCESS: 45 POINTS
Team success represents 45% of the entire award.
The 45 points are divided according to the importance of the competition.
A. World Cup / equivalent global national-team tournament — 15 points
Maximum: 15 points
This category evaluates the player's team's performance in the World Cup or the equivalent highest-level global national-team tournament for the relevant year.
Consider:
Winning the tournament
Reaching the final
Reaching the semifinals
Reaching the quarterfinals
Advancing from the group/initial stage
Player's actual participation and contribution
Important rule
A player does not automatically receive the full 15 points simply because their national team won.
The player's participation and contribution should influence the final score.
A player who played every important match should receive substantially more credit than a player who was part of the squad but barely played.

B. Continental Club Competitions — 12 points
Maximum: 12 points
Examples:
UEFA Champions League
UEFA Europa League
Copa Libertadores

CAF Champions League
CONCACAF Champions Cup
Other equivalent continental club championships
Consider:
Winning the competition
Reaching the final
Reaching semifinals/quarterfinals
Player's participation
Player's contribution to the team's progress
The continental competition should generally receive greater weight than an ordinary domestic competition because it involves stronger cross-national opposition.

C. National/Domestic Competitions — 10 points
Maximum: 10 points

This category should consider the player's team's achievements across the country's major domestic competitions.
The assessment should distinguish between:
League title

Player participation and contribution

D. Other Competitions — 8 points
Maximum: 8 points
This category captures important competitions that do not fit naturally into the previous three categories.
Examples can include:

FIFA Club World Cup
Community Shield
Other officially recognized super cups
Other significant official competitions
The exact competitions included should be documented each year so the methodology remains consistent.

5. CATEGORY III — MAN OF THE MATCH VOLUME: 10 POINTS
Maximum: 10 points
This measures the total number of officially recorded MOTM awards during the evaluation period.
Formula:
MOTM Volume Score = player's MOTM total relative to the field × 10
The same MOTM source/database should be used for all players whenever possible.
Important consistency rule
Do not mix sources arbitrarily.
For example, do not use:
FotMob for one player
SofaScore for another
UEFA awards for another
unless the methodology explicitly allows it.
Ideally, use one consistent MOTM database covering all relevant competitions.

6. CATEGORY IV — MAN OF THE MATCH EFFICIENCY: 10 POINTS
Maximum: 10 points
This measures how frequently the player was named MOTM relative to the number of matches played.
Formula
MOTM Efficiency Rate = MOTM Awards ÷ Matches Played × 100
Example:
Player A:
20 MOTMs
60 matches
20 ÷ 60 = 33.3% MOTM rate
Player B:
15 MOTMs
30 matches
15 ÷ 30 = 50.0% MOTM rate
Player A has greater MOTM volume.
Player B has greater MOTM efficiency.
This prevents players who simply play more matches from automatically dominating the MOTM category.
Efficiency score
The efficiency rate should then be converted into a score out of 10, relative to the eligible players being evaluated.

8. MATCHES PLAYED — IMPORTANT CONTROL VARIABLE
Matches played is not itself a scoring category.

9. TIME PERIOD
The award must define its evaluation period before calculating scores.
For example:
Evaluation period: 1 January 2026 – 31 December 2026
or, if using a football-season model:
Evaluation period: 1 August 2025 – 31 July 2026
Do not change the period from player to player.
Every player must be evaluated over the same period.

11. FINAL FORMULA
The final Player of the Year score is:

12. INTERPRETING THE SCORE
The score should be treated as a comparative ranking system, not an absolute statement about a player's ability.
A player with:
90–100 = exceptionally strong Player-of-the-Year season
80–89 = outstanding season
70–79 = elite season
60–69 = very strong season
50–59 = strong season
Below 50 = comparatively weaker among the evaluated candidates
These ranges are descriptive guidelines, not additional scoring rules.

13. IMPORTANT PRINCIPLES
Principle 1 — Team success matters, but trophies alone do not decide the award.
A player should receive credit for team success according to their actual participation and contribution.
Principle 2 — Individual statistics matter.
A player should not win simply because they were part of a successful team.
Principle 3 — Match impact matters.
MOTM awards attempt to measure whether the player actually made the difference in individual matches.
Principle 4 — Efficiency matters.
A player who produces exceptional match impact in fewer appearances should not automatically lose to someone who simply accumulated more appearances.
Principle 5 — Discipline matters.
Repeated yellow and red cards reduce a player's final score.
Principle 6 — Position matters.
Statistics must be interpreted according to the player's position.
Principle 7 — Strength of competition matters.
Performances against elite opposition should be appropriately contextualized.
Principle 8 — Do not change the rules after seeing the results.
The scoring methodology should be established before the final ranking is calculated.

15. FINAL RANKING
After calculating all scores:
1st place = highest score
2nd place = second-highest score
3rd place = third-highest score
and so forth.
If two players have the same final score, use the following tie-breakers:
Higher Team Success score
Higher Personal Statistics score
Higher MOTM Efficiency
Higher MOTM Volume
Higher Discipline score
The tie-breakers do not change the 100-point scoring system; they only determine ordering when the final scores are equal.

THE CORE PHILOSOPHY
The metric is designed around one central question:
Who was the most complete and impactful football player of the year, considering what they achieved with their team, what they personally produced, how frequently they decided matches, and how disciplined they were?
The methodology must remain the same from year to year. Only the player data changes.
